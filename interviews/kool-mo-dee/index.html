Kool Mo Dee Has StarPower

Hip-hop pioneer and member of the Treacherous Three

Hip-hop pioneer Kool Mo Dee on Michael Jackson, his mother's songs and why music is a language without words.

The Story

Kool Mo Dee helped write the early rules of hip-hop as a member of the Treacherous Three. On StarPower Collective, he went back further, to the records that shaped him before he ever picked up a mic.

One song stopped him cold: Michael Jackson's "Rock with You." He couldn't believe a single record could carry street credibility, win over pop radio and still work for hip-hop all at once. For a young Black boy, he said, it made anything feel possible.

His musical education started at home. His mother loved Dionne Warwick and Aretha Franklin, and she told him stories about Frankie Lymon and the Teenagers, singing the songs to him a cappella. Through the family he also heard Harry Belafonte, calypso, Miriam Makeba and African chants, music he felt long before he understood the words.

Kool Mo Dee is honest that he doesn't love all music. He loves the music he loves. But when a record hits, he says, it becomes a universal language, and the songs with something to say are the ones that stay with him.

In Kool Mo Dee's Words

“I didn't love music. I love the music I loved.”

“It's like you can feel stuff and communicate without speech.”
