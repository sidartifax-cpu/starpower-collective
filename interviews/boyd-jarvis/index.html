Boyd Jarvis Has StarPower

Producer, keyboardist and songwriter

Producer Boyd Jarvis on Crown Heights Affair, a Boston community center that sparked his love of fusion jazz, and making "The Music Got Me."

Every StarPower interview plays in rotation on Nu Soul Hwy Radio. Tune in over the next couple of weeks and you'll catch Boyd Jarvis's full show, with the music from their playlist.

The Story

Producer, keyboardist and songwriter Boyd Jarvis grew up between Brooklyn, Boston and Harlem, and every stop added something to his sound.

The first record he bought with his own money was Crown Heights Affair's "Super Rod." The band lived on the corner of his block, he used to listen to them rehearse, and he even took "Super Rod" as his graffiti tag.

In Boston's Roxbury, radio stations like WILD played deep Black music, from the Isley Brothers to Gil Scott-Heron. At the Harriet Tubman House, a community center in a four-story brownstone, he would climb to the top floor and listen to musicians rehearse. That's where he fell in love with fusion jazz, and with heroes like George Duke, Chick Corea, Stanley Clarke and Herbie Hancock.

Back in New York, he started spinning and buying records around 1976. His first professional record grew out of an instrumental and became "The Music Got Me," released by Prelude after he and his partner were already playing their instrumentals on WBLS.
